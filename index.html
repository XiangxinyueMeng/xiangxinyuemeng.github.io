01 / PHYSICOCHEMICAL EVOLUTION

When particles change their state.

I investigate how humidity, chemical composition, and atmospheric aging govern particle phase state. My first-author studies combine field measurements of phase transitions during haze with controlled experiments on gasoline vehicle emissions, linking changes in particle properties to secondary aerosol formation.

02 / HYGROSCOPICITY & CLOUDS

Connecting chemistry to cloud formation.

I lead investigations of aerosol hygroscopicity and cloud-forming properties in the southeastern United States using a year-long record of aerosol composition and cloud condensation nuclei (CCN) observations at the ARM Bankhead National Forest site. I examine how organosulfate formation affects water uptake and cloud activation. Interpretable machine learning and measurement-constrained parameterizations help identify the chemical and meteorological drivers and quantify their influence on CCN concentrations.

Ongoing research · manuscript in preparation · not yet published

03 / AIRBORNE EMERGING CONTAMINANTS

Making the unseen measurable.

I lead the development and field application of real-time methods for measuring particle-phase per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) with a high-resolution time-of-flight aerosol mass spectrometer (HR-TOF-AMS). Laboratory-validated fluorinated tracer ions enable detection of PFAS-containing submicron particles. By integrating aerosol composition, liquid water, and meteorological observations, I investigate the controls on PFAS partitioning, particle-phase retention, and atmospheric fate.
