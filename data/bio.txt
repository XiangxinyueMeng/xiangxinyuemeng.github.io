Atmospheric particles connect local emissions to broader changes in clouds, climate, and environmental quality. Dr. Xiangxinyue Meng studies how these particles evolve using laboratory experiments, field observations, and complementary mass spectrometric techniques. Her research examines how particle size, molecular composition, phase state, and interactions with water influence gas-particle partitioning, hygroscopicity, and cloud activation.

Building on this work, she develops online measurements of airborne emerging contaminants, including PFAS, to characterize their composition and dynamics in real time. These measurements help reveal how atmospheric processes govern contaminant transport, transformation, and fate as energy use, industrial practices, and chemical production change. Her broader goal is to connect particle-scale mechanisms with regional and Earth-system processes, improving understanding of the atmosphere's roles in climate, environmental quality, and human health.
